# Script Structures for @sirakinb

Pick the structure that fits the story. Never use the same structure as the previous script. Name the structure at the top of every script.

## 1. Yap Framework (tools, tutorials, "how I use X")
Calm Hook → Visual Proof → Benefit Re-Hook → Retention Statement → Named Steps → Save Bait Interrupt → CTA
- Calm Hook: say it plainly, no hype voice.
- Visual Proof: show the thing working within 5 seconds.
- Benefit Re-Hook: restate what the viewer gets.
- Retention Statement: why to stay to the end.
- Named Steps: give steps names, not numbers.
- Save Bait Interrupt: "save this, you'll need it when..." mid-video.

## 2. Shot-by-Shot (demos, green screen news, anything with visuals)
Numbered parts, each with ON SCREEN / VISUAL / SAY. The visual carries the story; the voice adds what the screen can't.

## 3. Timeline Cold Open (events with drama: IPOs, launches, lawsuits)
Open on the most dramatic moment, then rewind: "Thursday... Friday... Tuesday." Story beats, not a list. End on what it means for the viewer.

## 4. Contrarian Take (when everyone has the same headline)
"Everyone's saying X. Here's what I actually think." One argument, built with evidence, ending on his position.

## 5. Claim vs Receipt (benchmark launches, company claims)
Read the company's claim, then show the receipt. Alternate claim, receipt, claim, receipt. Ends on the verdict.

## 6. Then vs Now (shifts, trends, "the old way is dying")
Paint the old way in detail, flip to the new way, land on what to do now.

## 7. I Tried It (only when he actually used it)
First person, specific moment, what surprised him, verdict. Strongest format when it's true.

## 8. Numbered List (use sparingly, at most 1 in 4 scripts)

## Banned crutch lines (they made every script sound the same)
- "Three things. The third one..." or any "the last one is the one" teaser
- "Now the catch." / "But here's the catch."
- "But think about what that changes."
- Ending on a trailing "..." loop every time. Use a loop ending only when it truly completes the hook sentence, and not in back-to-back scripts.
